# gt-skin-figure and gt-skin-shared (Talk 13, Skin effect)

`gt-skin-figure/` builds the interactive figure served as `src/static/teardowns/skin-effect.html`: an imaginary optical bench for the photonic quantum walk of Wang, Xiao, Longhi and Xue (Nature Communications 2026, doi:10.1038/s41467-026-78483-z, CC BY NC ND 4.0). A photon source and state preparation optics feed one raised step of the walk (beam displacers and the loss plate HM, a dump for lost light, a flip mount for dephasing, coin wave plates and a q plate for the shift) and a bank of 17 detector channels whose count columns show where the surviving light lands; sliders set the loss γ, dephasing η, amplitude damping μ (before or after the loss) and the number of steps, and a tour steps through the paper's settings. It is an anatomy kit Figure (isometric SVG, no WebGL) and an original drawing that follows the order of optics in the paper's text and Methods, not its Fig. 1. Every number comes from `gt-skin-shared/walk.mjs`, my density matrix model written from the paper's equations (one step U = S C(θ) M, θ = π/4, start at x = 0 with coin (|0⟩ + i|1⟩)/√2, dephasing and damping as Kraus pairs, probabilities rescaled to the survivors each step, as the paper does); its long run drifts match the paper's Eq. 1 to 3 and its fitted slopes (0.71 and 0.25 at γ = 0.4, 0.745 at γ = 0.854, 0.78 and 0.87 at γ = 0.93, and 0.68, 0.60, 0.25 and 0.78, 0.79, 0.87 in Fig. 4), and the plate angles shown are the Methods formulas. The bank of 17 detectors is drawn for clarity (the real setup reads one position at a time). Build with `node teardowns/gt-skin-figure/build.mjs` from the repo root (the kit is read from `ANATOMY_KIT`, default `/home/claude/anatomy-ref`; `--dark` writes a dark check copy beside the script, and the kit's geometry audit runs on every build). The poster `src/assets/new-skin-poster.png` (1600 × 900) shows the bench at γ = 0.93 with full dephasing. The Talk 13 page charts (Chart A heat maps, Chart C bars) use values precomputed with the same `walk.mjs`.
