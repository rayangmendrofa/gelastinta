Rebuilding the InMoov robot hand

What happens when someone who just finished a dissertation on prosthetic hands gets a 3D printer: an open-source robot hand, printed and assembled piece by piece.

01 — The story

Why rebuild someone else’s hand?

Finishing my BEng dissertation on a modified prosthetic hand didn’t end my interest in hands. Between 2023 and 2024, with a 3D printer newly on my desk, I kept researching hand prosthetics by rebuilding one of the best-known open-source robot hands: the hand of the InMoov robot.

InMoov is an open-source, life-size, 3D-printable humanoid robot, started in January 2012 by French sculptor and designer Gaël Langevin, who shares the parts so anyone can print and build one. Its hand works much like ours: servo motors in the forearm pull on braided fishing-line “tendons” that curl each finger, while springs keep the tendons under tension.

Printing and assembling a proven design was my way of seeing how a working hand comes together, from how the tendons are routed through each finger to how the servos pull them, and of comparing it with the hand I had built for my dissertation.

Where it started: my BEng prosthetic hand →

Credit where it’s due

I did not design this hand. The InMoov hand is Gaël Langevin’s work; I printed and assembled it purely out of curiosity and as a hobby after getting my 3D printer.

Shoutout

Gaël Langevin, creator of InMoov

To Gaël and the InMoov community, for keeping the designs open. It’s exactly the kind of project that turns a new 3D printer into a classroom.
