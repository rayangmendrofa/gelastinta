My own drawing of an imaginary bench instrument, built on the paper’s numbers. The real scan is a whole head in the 3T Connectome scanner (300 mT/m gradients, 2 mm voxels, 7 b values from 200 to 8,000 s/mm² plus b = 0 images, 8 ms pulses 24 ms apart). In the cube, each water molecule carries a little arrow: the first gradient pulse turns it by an amount set by where the molecule is along the coils, the second turns it back by where it is now. Molecules that stayed put end up pointing the same way (signal kept); molecules that wandered do not (signal lost). The walk is a toy simulation, slowed right down, and the cells are a cartoon whose changes are magnified ten times. Dials: MD on its own post (one full turn = 10 % change), then soma, neurite and extracellular fractions (one full turn = 0.04 of the signal). Values are group means read off Fig 3 of Griffa et al., PLOS Biology 24(6): e3003861 (2026), CC BY 4.0, so the third decimal is approximate. The real training took about 15 to 20 minutes; here a block lasts under half a second.
